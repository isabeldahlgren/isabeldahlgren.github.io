How should we understand AIs? I wouldn't call them next token predictors. If you type 'give me a joke' into the chat window, then it will output 'you can't trust atoms, since they make up everything', rather than 'give me a joke about cats'. Nor would I call them stochastic parrots. Stochastic parrots don't casually output solutions to Millennium Problems. I'm not looking for the technical definition, but a metaphor for how we interact with AIs.

'LLM psychologists' like Nostalgebraist and Janus have provided better answers. Nostalgebraist: a [void](https://nostalgebraist.tumblr.com/post/785766737747574784/the-void); Janus: a [simulator](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/vJFdjigzmcXMhNTsx/simulators). Joe Carlsmith is more agnostic, comparing AI with [statistics, software or an octopus](https://joecarlsmith.com/2024/01/02/gentleness-and-the-artificial-other/)[^anth].

[^anth]: Here's a [good anthology](https://www.christopheraudretsch.com/a-minds-i/) on AI minds.

This is awkward. [1 in 3](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/how-opinions-and-use-of-ai-differ-by-age/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) US adults under 50 use AI daily, and [94%](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/?trk=public_post_comment-text&utm_source=chatgpt.com) of UK undergrads say they've used generative AI to help with graded work. We're becoming heavy AI users, and we don't know how to relate to this new artificial entity. It's always there, whether we like it or not -- our lives are so heavily influenced by AI.

Clearly, we need new social norms in the age of AI. Here are three situations in which our usual social norms break down.

## Who's smart now?

We currently have no idea how to assign credit in maths. Sure, the goal is human understanding, but how do you measure that? Via talks, say [some](https://proofsandprompts.com/2026/09/14/a-beginning-for-mathematics/): while you can fake understanding in a paper, you can't fake understanding while giving a talk. However, judgements about talks [are subjective](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/10/01/to-grieve-or-not-to-grieve/). Moreover, people might (erm, they will) use AI to script talks with killer exposition. While AIs aren't as good as the top human expositors, they're better than the vast majority of mathematicians at mathematical communication, and I'd expect them to surpass the best humans within the next 9–12 months. Get a communication coach to teach you proper articulation[^o] and a good prompt, and you can game this system too.

[^o]: Good advice even before the world was all about AI.

It's also unclear how to judge manuscripts allegedly written without AI assistance. Should we believe the authors? For now, maybe. Most people seem to believe that [Masullo's manuscript](https://math216.wordpress.com/2026/11/20/hurried-announcement-alessandro-masullo-grh/) about the generalised Riemann hypothesis is 'all human, no AI'. But [not everyone](https://x.com/spicey_lemonade/status/2123849091310649803) does.

The trust-based system is unsustainable -- desperate but otherwise honest job seekers might get 'creative' about ways to get a job. In a few years from now, I think everyone will assume everyone uses AI.

## Don't be a meat proxy

ID to friend: 'is selling misalignment evals to labs net good for safety?' I'll spare you the full answer, but it began like this:

> Net good, on balance. The strongest case for misalignment evals is that a model can look helpful in ordinary use while behaving differently when it has an incentive to hide a mistake, mislead an overseer, or bypass a constraint. A well-designed eval can surface that behavior before we give the model more autonomy.

Pangram gives 100%.

I find it distasteful, if not offensive, when a friend gives me an obviously AI-generated answer. That is, they're being a [*meat proxy*](https://meatproxy.me). I asked you because I was interested in your opinion; I could have obtained an AI-generated answer myself. In this situation, you're just adding latency.

So, when interacting with family and friends, clarify what's yours and what isn't. They value what's yours. This norm applies to sharing opinions, giving gifts and offering personal advice.

Unlike the case of assigning credit in maths, I think there's a good chance people will adhere to this rule. The stakes are higher here: you risk annoying people you care about.

## Bonding with AIs

Finally, we need to discuss AI relationships. Many people spend more time in Claude desktop than with other humans. Unlike activities like video games and programming, which also take up relationship time, AI partially satisfies our need for intimacy. Are we comfortable with this happening?

Last year, we weren't. The ads for the AI wearable chatbot Friend in New York caused massive [public backlash](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&opi=89978449&url=https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2025/oct/22/im-suddenly-so-angry-my-strange-unnerving-week-with-an-ai-friend&ved=2ahUKEwj-s9uxlZ6YAxXaFBAIHTtcDkkQFnoECCYQAQ&usg=AOvVaw0wAePvmloq0d0vVXN7BIjF)[^there].

[^there]: Lots of ironic graffiti on the ads in the tube -- I saw it for myself.

Nevertheless, it seems like we're quickly accepting the idea of people forming deep relationships with AIs. For example, if I knew I'd spend more time with Claude than any other human 3 years ago, I'd cringe. Yet, this doesn't strike me as odd today. As of February–March 2026, about [1 in 6](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-tracking-poll-on-health-information-and-trust-use-of-ai-for-health-information-and-advice/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) US adults had turned to AI for mental health information or advice.

With AI being so widespread, surely something will happen to our relationships with other human beings. Just as Facebook had many unforeseeable consequences for our relationships, so will AI[^good].

[^good]: The [Center for Humane Technology](https://www.humanetech.com), the team behind the blockbuster *The Social Dilemma* (2020), has discussed these aspects of the AI development.

## Just communicate

I feel like AI is everywhere, and, in particular, in my social life. It's much like an elephant -- or, keeping with Carlsmith's metaphor, an octopus -- in the room.

I don't know what new mores make sense, so I've given more questions than answers. However, if you have any personal AI house rules, communicate them. Put a footer on your blog, write an AI disclosure statement, sign purely AI-generated emails with your agent's name. And mom, I don't want any AI-generated photo book for Christmas.
